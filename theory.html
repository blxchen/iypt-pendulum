MODEL NOTES
07 / THEORY

Two modes. One trace.

The Y suspension gives the bob two different effective pendulum lengths. Their frequency ratio controls whether and when the top-view trajectory closes.

01 · GEOMETRY

Construct the Y

Two upper strings of length S run from supports separated by d to a junction. A lower string of length ℓ carries the bob.

h = √(S² − (d/2)²)
Lₓ = ℓ
Lᵧ = h + ℓ

Choose S > d/2 so the upper strings can meet.

02 · FREQUENCIES

Small-angle model

For ideal strings and small release angles, the two modes oscillate at different natural frequencies.

ωₓ = √(g/Lₓ)
ωᵧ = √(g/Lᵧ)
ωₓ / ωᵧ = √(Lᵧ/Lₓ)

Mass does not affect these ideal frequencies. It is retained as an experimental parameter.

03 · TRAJECTORY

Lissajous motion

x(t) = Aₓe−γₓtcos(ωₓt)
y(t) = Aᵧe−γᵧtcos(ωᵧt + φ)

In the app, Aₓ = Lₓ sin(αₓ), Aᵧ = Lᵧ sin(αᵧ). The phase offset φ changes shape; damping shrinks the trace over time. A rational frequency ratio p:q produces a closed ideal trace after p X cycles and q Y cycles.

04 · INVESTIGATION

Experimental procedure

  1. Measure support spacing and both string lengths.
  2. Release the bob with small, measured X and Y angles.
  3. Track top-view bob positions with a calibrated camera.
  4. Compare measured frequencies and traces against the ideal model.
  5. Change one parameter at a time; repeat each run.

Use the CSV upload on the analysis page to compare your measured trajectory visually.

05 · SCOPE

Model limits and references

This is a small-angle, two-mode model with independent exponential damping. The 3D display reconstructs a constrained Y for visualization. It does not integrate a fully coupled 3D string and junction system, string elasticity, knot friction, or slack. At large release angles and with strong damping, the displayed geometry can differ from real apparatus motion. Manual ratio mode is a pattern explorer; its frequency need not agree with the selected geometry.
