MODEL & METHOD

The physics behind the trace

A two-coordinate nonlinear model for ideal, taut Y-shaped strings.

01 / GEOMETRY

Fixed string lengths

Upper support points lie at (±d/2, 0, 0). Each upper string has length S. The junction moves along a circle of radius h in the y–z plane; the lower string has length ℓ.

h = √(S² − (d/2)²)
J = (0, h sin θ, −h cos θ)
B = (ℓ sin φ, (h + ℓ cos φ) sin θ, −(h + ℓ cos φ) cos θ)

Every simulated position preserves all three string lengths exactly.

02 / EQUATIONS

Coupled motion

For a point bob of mass m and a massless junction, T = ½m[ℓ²φ̇² + R²θ̇²], V = −mgR cos θ and R = h + ℓ cos φ. The Euler–Lagrange equations are:

φ̈ = −(R/ℓ) sin φ · θ̇² − (g/ℓ) sin φ cos θ
θ̈ = (2ℓ sin φ/R) φ̇θ̇ − (g/R) sin θ

Optional damping adds −2γₓφ̇ and −2γᵧθ̇ respectively. A fixed-step fourth-order Runge–Kutta solver integrates these equations.

03 / SMALL ANGLES

Where Lissajous figures come from

ωₓ ≈ √(g/ℓ)
ωᵧ ≈ √(g/(h + ℓ))
x ≈ ℓφ,   y ≈ (h + ℓ)θ

These frequencies are shown as reference values. At larger angles the nonlinear frequencies change, modes couple and the trace can drift. Mass cancels from the ideal equations; it still changes energy and real damping forces.

04 / EXPERIMENT

Compare with the apparatus

  1. Measure S, d, ℓ and the initial release angles.
  2. Film the bob from above and calibrate pixels to metres.
  3. Compare the measured trace and frequency ratio with this ideal model.
  4. Check string stretch, junction mass, friction and slack when the curves disagree.

The model assumes a massless junction, taut inextensible strings, a point bob and fixed supports. It does not claim to reproduce every detail of the demonstration video.
